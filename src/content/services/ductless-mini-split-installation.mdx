Some rooms never feel right. Too hot in summer, too cold in winter, or just disconnected from the rest of the home's comfort. Extending ductwork is sometimes costly, impractical, or impossible. That's where ductless mini-splits can make sense.

JT's can help you talk through whether a mini-split fits the space, what capacity makes sense, and how the install process works before you commit.

## Where mini-split installation can help

- Finished garages, workshops, or home offices that do not tie into central HVAC.
- Additions, bonus rooms, sunrooms, or converted attics where running ducts is not practical.
- Guest suites or in-law spaces that need independent comfort control.
- Rooms that are always uncomfortable no matter what the thermostat says.
- Homes where you want zone control without a full duct retrofit.

## What to expect from the install conversation

We will ask about the space. How big is the room, what is the insulation like, how many exterior walls, what windows does it have, and what comfort problems are you trying to solve.

Then we will talk through mini-split options that fit the heating and cooling load, explain where the indoor and outdoor units would go, and walk through what the install involves.

You will know what the system can and cannot do, what install work is required, and whether it makes sense for your situation before anything moves forward.

## How a ductless mini-split works

A mini-split system has two main parts. An outdoor compressor unit that handles the heating and cooling work, and one or more indoor air handlers mounted on the wall or ceiling of the space you want to condition.

A small line set connects the indoor and outdoor units through a hole in the wall. The indoor unit distributes heated or cooled air directly into the room. No ductwork required.

Each indoor unit has its own remote or control, so you can set the temperature independently from the rest of the house.

## When installation makes sense

Mini-splits work well when the room is defined, the space has a clear heating and cooling need, and running ducts would be expensive or disruptive.

They are not always the right answer. If the main system is undersized or failing, or if the whole home has comfort issues, fixing the central HVAC may be the better path. We will talk through both options honestly.

## Install process and timing

Once you decide to move forward, we will schedule the install, confirm equipment delivery, and walk you through what happens on install day.

The process involves mounting the indoor unit, setting the outdoor compressor, running the line set and electrical connections, and testing the system to confirm it heats and cools correctly.

Most single-zone installs can be completed in a day. Multi-zone systems or complicated layouts may take longer. We will let you know the timeline before work begins.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know what size mini-split I need?
It depends on the room size, insulation, windows, ceiling height, and how much heating or cooling load the space has. We will ask questions about the space and help you choose a unit that fits the actual load without over-sizing or under-sizing.

### Can I install a mini-split myself?
The indoor and outdoor units need to be mounted, the line set needs to be run and connected, refrigerant lines need to be evacuated and charged, and electrical work needs to meet code. Most homeowners are better off hiring a licensed installer to handle the work correctly.

### Will a mini-split heat and cool?
Yes. Most ductless mini-splits are heat pumps. They provide both heating and cooling in the same unit. Some models work down to very cold outdoor temperatures, but performance can drop in extreme cold. We will talk through what makes sense for West Michigan winters.

### How long does a mini-split install take?
A single-zone install typically takes a full day. Multi-zone systems or installs with complicated routing or electrical work may take longer. We will give you a clear timeline before scheduling the work.

### Do I need to do anything to maintain a mini-split?
The indoor unit has a filter that should be cleaned regularly. The outdoor unit should be kept clear of debris, snow, and obstructions. Beyond that, seasonal check-ups can help catch small issues before they turn into bigger problems.

### Can I add more indoor units later?
It depends on the outdoor unit capacity and how the system was originally installed. Some outdoor units can handle multiple indoor zones. If you think you might want to add zones later, let us know during the planning conversation so we can size the outdoor unit accordingly.
