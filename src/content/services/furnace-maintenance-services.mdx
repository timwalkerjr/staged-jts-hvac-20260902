A furnace that runs fine in October can fail on the coldest night of January. JT's offers seasonal furnace maintenance to check the parts that matter, look for signs of trouble, and help you decide whether anything needs attention before the temperature drops.

This is not a high-pressure inspection. It is a practical seasonal check for homeowners who want to know their heating system is ready.

## What a furnace maintenance visit includes

JT's will look at the parts that keep your furnace running safely and efficiently through a Michigan winter.

- Check the ignition system and flame sensor for reliable startup.
- Inspect the heat exchanger for visible cracks or signs of wear.
- Test the blower motor and check airflow through the system.
- Look at the air filter and recommend replacement if needed.
- Check thermostat operation and temperature accuracy.
- Inspect venting and flue connections for safe exhaust.
- Test safety controls, limit switches, and shutoff functions.

If something looks off, we will explain what was found and what makes sense as a next step. Maintenance does not mean selling you parts you do not need.

## When to schedule furnace maintenance

Fall is the practical time. Before the first real cold snap, before the furnace is running daily, and before you find out there is a problem when you need heat most.

Maintenance also makes sense if the furnace is older, if you have noticed odd behavior during the last heating season, or if you want a clear answer about whether the system is ready for another winter.

## What happens if something needs attention

We will talk through what was found. If a part is worn, if airflow is weak, or if something should be monitored, you will know before we do any work. Some issues need repair right away. Others can wait or just need watching. We will help you decide what makes sense for your situation.

### Honest guidance
Not every furnace needs work after a maintenance visit. If the system looks ready, we will tell you that. If something should be addressed, we will explain why and what the options are.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often should a furnace be checked?
Once a year is standard. Most homeowners schedule maintenance in the fall before the heating season starts. If the furnace is older or has had issues, a seasonal check becomes more important.

### What if the furnace seems fine?
Maintenance is about catching problems before they show up as symptoms. A furnace can run normally in mild weather and fail when it is working harder during a cold stretch. A seasonal check helps you know whether the system is truly ready.

### Will maintenance prevent all breakdowns?
No. Maintenance reduces the chance of surprise failures, but parts can still wear out or fail between visits. What it does is give you a clearer picture of the system's condition and help you avoid problems that could have been caught early.

### Do you try to sell repairs during maintenance?
We explain what we find. If something needs attention, we will tell you why and what the options are. If the furnace looks ready for the season, we will tell you that too. No pressure either way.

### Can you do maintenance on older furnaces?
Yes. Older systems benefit from seasonal checks because wear is more likely. If a furnace is nearing the end of its practical life, we will let you know what we see and help you decide whether maintenance, repair, or replacement planning makes more sense.

### What if something is found during the visit?
We will explain what it is, whether it is urgent, and what fixing it involves. Some issues can be addressed the same day if you want to move forward. Others can be scheduled later or monitored. You decide the next step after hearing the options.
