A heat pump handles both heating and cooling from one system. When the current one is aging, unreliable, or causing year-round comfort complaints, replacement may be the practical path. JT's can help you talk through equipment options, efficiency levels, compatibility with your home, and what installation involves.

## When heat pump replacement makes sense

- The system is older and repair costs are starting to add up.
- Winter heating performance is weak or the home never feels warm enough.
- Summer cooling is uneven, inconsistent, or struggles during peak heat.
- You want better efficiency or lower monthly utility bills.
- The equipment is noisy, unreliable, or requires frequent attention.

## What to expect during the conversation

We will start with what is happening now. Tell us about comfort problems, repair history, age of the equipment, and what you are hoping for from a new system. JT's will ask about the home's size, layout, insulation, and ductwork to help identify what will work well.

You will hear the practical options, not a one-size pitch. We will talk through efficiency levels, equipment brands we work with, timing, and cost ranges before any decision is made. If the current system can be repaired affordably and still has useful life, we will tell you that too.

## Installation details

A heat pump installation involves replacing the outdoor unit, the indoor air handler or coil, refrigerant lines, electrical connections, thermostat wiring, and condensate drainage. Depending on the home and existing setup, ductwork adjustments or electrical upgrades may also be part of the plan.

We will walk through what the installation requires before scheduling. You will know the timeline, what access we need, and what to expect during the work.

## Choosing the right size and efficiency

An undersized heat pump will struggle to keep up. An oversized one will cycle too often and waste energy. JT's will help you sort out the right capacity based on your home's heating and cooling load, not guesswork.

Efficiency ratings matter for long-term comfort and utility bills. We can explain the difference between baseline and higher-efficiency models and help you decide what fits your budget and comfort goals.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know if I need a new heat pump or just repair?
Age, repair history, and performance all matter. If the system is newer and the issue is isolated, repair may be the right call. If it is older, less efficient, and requiring frequent fixes, replacement often makes more sense long-term. We will help you sort that out based on what is happening.

### Can a heat pump handle West Michigan winters?
Modern heat pumps are much more capable in cold weather than older models. Many systems now include backup heat or hybrid setups to handle extreme cold snaps. We will talk through how different options perform in winter and what makes sense for your heating needs.

### How long does a heat pump installation take?
Most residential heat pump installations take one to two days depending on the complexity of the job, ductwork needs, and electrical work. We will give you a clearer timeline once we understand your home and the scope of the install.

### Will a new heat pump lower my utility bills?
A higher-efficiency heat pump uses less energy than an older, worn-out system. How much you save depends on the efficiency level you choose, how the old system was performing, and how you use the equipment. We can talk through realistic expectations during the estimate.

### What brands do you install?
JT's works with reliable equipment brands that perform well in West Michigan conditions. We will explain what we recommend and why based on your situation, budget, and comfort goals.

### Do I need to upgrade my electrical panel?
Some heat pump installations require electrical upgrades, especially in older homes. We will assess what your home has during the estimate and let you know if any electrical work is needed before the install.
