An HVAC inspection is a full look at the heating and cooling equipment before a problem forces your hand. JT's can check furnace, A/C, heat pump, or ductless components to help you know what is happening with the system and whether anything should be addressed now or later.

## When an inspection makes sense

- You bought a home and want to know the condition of the HVAC system.
- The equipment is older and you want a realistic picture before it fails.
- Something feels off but there is no clear breakdown yet.
- You are planning a home improvement project and need to know if the system will hold up.
- The last service company gave vague answers and you want a second opinion.
- You need documentation for a real estate transaction or insurance claim.

## What the inspection covers

JT's will walk through the equipment and look at the parts that matter for safe operation, reliability, and comfort. The goal is to give you a clear understanding of what you have and what might be coming.

### Heating equipment
Check furnace ignition, burner assembly, heat exchanger condition, blower motor, and airflow performance. Look for signs of wear, corrosion, or safety concerns that could lead to failure.

### Cooling equipment
Inspect outdoor unit condition, refrigerant levels, evaporator coil cleanliness, condensate drain function, and electrical connections. Check for weak airflow, freezing, or components that are nearing the end of their lifespan.

### Airflow and comfort
Look at ductwork condition where accessible, return air setup, filter condition, and thermostat operation. Identify uneven temperatures or airflow problems that affect comfort.

### Safety and efficiency
Check carbon monoxide risk, gas connections, electrical safety, and whether the system is running longer or harder than it should. Flag anything that could become urgent or expensive if left alone.

## What you get after the inspection

After walking through the system, JT's will talk through what was found. You will know what is in good shape, what needs attention soon, and what can wait. No pressure to schedule work immediately. The goal is to help you plan and decide what makes sense for your timeline and budget.

If repairs or replacements are recommended, we will explain why and what happens if you wait. If everything looks solid, you will know that too.

## Inspection versus maintenance

An inspection is a one-time assessment to understand the condition of the system. Maintenance is seasonal service to keep the equipment running well. Both serve different purposes. If you are not sure which fits your situation, tell us what you are looking for and we will help you sort it out.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does an inspection take?
Most full HVAC inspections take one to two hours depending on the system type, accessibility, and what is found during the visit.

### Will the inspection tell me how much life is left in the system?
We can give you a realistic assessment based on the equipment age, condition, and wear patterns. Exact lifespan predictions are not possible, but you will know if replacement should be on your radar soon or if the system looks like it has years left.

### Can you inspect just the furnace or just the A/C?
Yes. If you only need one side of the system checked, let us know when you reach out. We can focus the inspection on heating, cooling, or both depending on what you need.

### What if the inspection finds problems?
We will explain what was found, what the risk is if you wait, and what the repair or replacement options look like. You decide what happens next. There is no obligation to schedule work during the visit.

### Do you provide a written report?
Yes. After the inspection, you will receive documentation of what was checked and what was found. This can be helpful for your own records, real estate transactions, or planning future service.

### Is an inspection the same as a tune-up?
No. A tune-up includes cleaning, adjustments, and preventive service to keep the system running well. An inspection is a diagnostic look at the equipment to assess condition and identify concerns. If you are not sure which you need, describe what you are hoping to accomplish and we will recommend the right fit.
