Most water heaters sit in basements or utility rooms doing their job without much attention. That works until it does not.

JT's can schedule a seasonal check to look at the condition of the tank, the safety components, and the parts that contribute to reliable hot water. We will let you know if the tank looks ready to keep going or if something needs attention before it turns into a bigger issue.

## What gets checked during water heater maintenance

- Check the tank for visible rust, corrosion, or early signs of leaks around fittings and valves.
- Test the temperature and pressure relief valve to confirm it can release pressure safely.
- Inspect the anode rod if accessible—the part that helps slow internal corrosion and extends tank life.
- Flush sediment buildup from the bottom of the tank to improve efficiency and reduce strain.
- Check burner operation, pilot light, and gas connections on gas water heaters.
- Inspect electrical elements, thermostats, and wiring on electric water heaters.
- Look at water lines, shut-off valves, and drain pans for leaks or wear.

## When water heater maintenance makes sense

Maintenance is practical for tanks that are working but aging, tanks in homes with hard water, or when you want to know the condition before planning around the house.

If the water heater is already leaking, making loud noises, or not producing hot water, that is a repair or replacement conversation, not a maintenance visit.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often should a water heater be checked?
Most manufacturers recommend annual maintenance, especially if the water heater is past the five-year mark or if you have hard water that accelerates sediment buildup.

### Can maintenance prevent a water heater from failing?
Maintenance can catch early warning signs and extend the life of the tank, but it cannot stop a tank from aging. If the tank is nearing the end of its expected lifespan or showing rust and corrosion, replacement may still be the practical next step.

### What happens if sediment is not flushed?
Sediment settles at the bottom of the tank and reduces efficiency. It can also cause the tank to overheat, create rumbling noises, and shorten the life of the water heater.

### Can you replace the anode rod during maintenance?
If the anode rod is accessible and worn, we can talk through replacement. Some tank designs make anode rod service difficult or impractical, and we will let you know what makes sense for your setup.

### Does maintenance help with lukewarm water or slow recovery?
Flushing sediment and checking heating elements or burners can help with temperature and recovery issues, but if the tank is undersized for the home or the heating components are failing, repair or replacement may be the better path.
